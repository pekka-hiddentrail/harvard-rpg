# Harvard Concentrations — Requirements Reference

All concentrations researched across this conversation, in a consistent format.

---

## Applied Mathematics

Foundation + Breadth + Area of Application: 14-15 courses

* Foundation: 2-5 courses in calculus/linear algebra depending on starting point (Math Ma/Mb through 1a/1b, or straight into 21a/22a/21b)
* Breadth: courses spanning computation, probability, discrete and continuous math
* Area of Application: 5 courses in a field where math is substantively applied — functions as an open-ended track system (Economics, Government, Psychology, Astrophysics, Chemistry, Theoretical Neuroscience, Data Science, and Digital Humanities are all documented options)
* Honors: automatically satisfied with A– or higher in AM 115/215, or via an independent modeling project (AM 91r)
* Cannot be combined with another field as a joint concentration

## Chemistry

Basic Requirements: 12-14 courses (48-56 credits)

* General chemistry (2 courses)
* Inorganic chemistry (1 course: Chem 40 or equivalent)
* Organic chemistry (2 courses: Chem 20/30 or Chem 17/27)
* Physical chemistry (2 courses)
* Advanced laboratory (1 course)
* Chemistry with strong biological orientation (1 course)
* Math (Math 21a or equivalent; 21b strongly recommended)
* Physics (2 courses)
* Additional courses to reach 12 (13-14 if placement requires Math 1a/1b)
* No thesis required. Optional sophomore/junior tutorials (Chem 91r, 98r), SAT/UNS graded.

Honors Eligibility: 14-16 courses (56-64 credits)

* Same core as Basic, plus 2 additional advanced chemistry/biochem/related-field courses
* At least 4 courses numbered 100+
* Thesis optional even at honors level (via Chem 99r)
* No General Examination

## Classics

Classical Civilizations Track: 12 courses

* Two introductory civilization courses (CLS-STDY 97A, 97B)
* Six courses in Greek and/or Latin (2+ numbered 100 or above, including one capstone)
* One semester of Classics 98 (junior tutorial)
* Senior thesis required, with two semesters of Classics 99
* Lighter language emphasis than the Languages & Literatures track

Classical Languages and Literatures Track: 12 courses

* Same structure as Classical Civilizations Track
* Heavier emphasis on reading Greek/Latin literature in the original language

Joint Concentration in Ancient History (Greek and Roman): 7 courses (in Classics)

* Co-sponsored with the History Department
* One semester of Classical Studies 97a or 97b, one semester of Classics 98, two courses in Greek/Latin
* Functions as a de facto third track

---

## Comparative Literature

Concentration Requirements: 14 courses (56 credits)

* Sophomore Tutorial (COMPLIT 97)
* Junior Tutorial (COMPLIT 98A/98B), or an approved alternative
* Senior Tutorial (COMPLIT 99A/99B) — the thesis-writing course
* 3 Comparative Literature courses, each B- or above
* 3 courses in one or more non-English literature(s), each B- or above
* 3 electives from related departments (English, non-English literatures, Folklore & Mythology, Philosophy, Art/Film/Visual Studies, WGS, Linguistics, or additional language study)
* Senior thesis required of every concentrator — no opt-out
* Oral exam required (75 minutes, thesis defense + intellectual autobiography)
* No separate Basic/Honors split — this is a single, honors-only structure

## Computer Science

Basic Requirements: 11-14 courses (44-56 credits)

* Math prep (2-5 courses): calculus as needed, one linear algebra course, one probability course
* CS core (9 courses): Programming 1 & 2, Formal Reasoning, Systems, Computation and the World, Advanced CS (4 courses)
* No thesis required

Honors Requirements: 13-16 courses (52-64 credits)

* Same math prep as Basic
* CS core (11 courses): includes Intermediate Algorithms instead of Algorithms, a required Artificial Intelligence course, and 5 Advanced CS courses (vs. 4)
* Thesis optional, but required for high/highest honors

Joint Concentrations: 10-13 courses (40-52 credits, CS side)

* Same math prep as Basic
* Only 8 CS core courses needed instead of 9
* Thesis required, combining both fields, read by both departments

Mind, Brain, and Behavior (MBB) Track: 13-16 courses (52-64 credits)

* Honors-only program, affiliated with the university-wide MBB Initiative
* Same math prep as Honors
* CS core: only 8 courses (4 Advanced CS courses instead of 5, one CS 91r can count)
* Plus 3 dedicated MBB courses: Neuroscience 80, an approved MBB course outside CS, an approved MBB junior tutorial
* Thesis required — must be computationally-oriented and MBB-related

## East Asian Studies

Primary Concentration — Honors

* Requires senior thesis and senior tutorial
* Sophomore + junior tutorials required (EAS 97ab, EAS 98)
* Junior year: elect a country focus (usually driven by language choice), choose social-science or humanities tutorial track for that country
* Most concentrators spend 2-3 years on their chosen language (Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, or less-common options)

Primary Concentration — Non-Honors

* Same tutorial structure as Honors, without the thesis requirement

Joint Concentration, Language Track

* Combines EAS with another field; requires language study

Joint Concentration, Area Track

* Combines EAS with another field; no language courses required

Joint Concentration in East Asian History: 14 half-courses

* Co-sponsored with the History Department
* Honors-only; either EAS 99 or History 99 as senior thesis tutorial

Secondary Field, Language Track: 9 half-courses

* Includes sophomore tutorial (EAS 97ab)

Secondary Field, Area Track: 6 half-courses

* Includes sophomore tutorial (EAS 97ab); no language requirement

## Economics

Basic Requirements: 11 courses

* ECON 10a & 10b (Principles of Micro/Macro)
* MATH 1a (or equivalent)
* One statistics course (ECON 20, STAT 100/102/104/109/110, AM 101, MATH 154, or ECON 20)
* ECON 1010a/1011a (Intermediate Microeconomics) and ECON 1010b/1011b (Intermediate Macroeconomics), B- or higher required
* ECON 970 (Sophomore Tutorial, can be taken junior year)
* Econometrics (ECON 1123 or 1126)
* 3 additional electives, one requiring intermediate theory as prerequisite
* No thesis required

Honors — Thesis Track: 14 courses

* Same as Basic, plus multivariable calculus (Math 18A/21A or higher)
* Two semesters of ECON 985 (Senior Research Seminar)
* Completed senior thesis
* Honors exam (3-hour general exam: micro, macro, econometrics)
* Eligible for summa/magna/cum

Honors — Advanced Course Track (ACT): 14 courses

* Same as Basic, plus multivariable calculus
* Two additional courses instead of a thesis (one requiring intermediate theory)
* Honors exam
* Eligible for cum only

## Engineering Sciences

First-Year Track: no separate named sub-tracks confirmed

* ENG-SCI 51 and ENG-SCI 53 (Quantitative Physiology as a Basis for Engineering)
* LS 1A (or LPS A can also satisfy this)
* Math per placement: Ma, Mb, 1a, 1b; or 21a, 21b
* Physics: one of APPHY 50A/B, Physical Sciences 2/3, Physical Sciences 12a/12b, or Physics 15a/15b
* Students specialize via electives after the first year rather than through a formally branded track system

## English

Elective Program: 11-14 courses

* No thesis required
* All concentrators take three "Common Ground" courses

Honors Program: 14 courses

* Junior Tutorial required
* At least one 90-level seminar required
* Senior Thesis or Senior Project required (critical or creative)
* Concentration GPA cutoff varies by year (3.65 or below for Class of 2026)

Joint Concentration: 6-8 courses (English side)

* Thesis required, English as primary or allied field
* GPA cutoff of 3.60 or higher

## Government

Standard Track: 10 courses

* Gov 97 (Sophomore Tutorial)
* Methods Requirement: Gov 50 (Data), Gov 51, or an outside Stat course
* Subfield Requirement: one course each in Political Theory, Comparative Government, American Government, International Relations
* 3 Gov electives (4 if methods satisfied outside the department)
* One Gov 94 seminar
* No thesis, no senior tutorial required

Honors Track: 13 courses

* Same sophomore tutorial, methods, subfield, and elective requirements as Standard
* Seminar requirement doubles: one Gov 94 and one Research Practice Course (Gov 51, 62, or 63)
* Gov 99 (Senior Tutorial), two terms, SAT/UNS graded
* Thesis required — doesn't count as a course itself
* Oral exam required in certain circumstances

Informal Programs of Study (layered on top of standard requirements)

* Data Science, Tech Science, Political Economy, Public Policy, Law and Justice

## History of Art and Architecture (HAA)

General HAA Concentration

* Students select a major field focus (African, Ancient, Byzantine, Chinese, Islamic, Medieval, Modern/Contemporary, Renaissance, etc.) — an area of emphasis, not a formally separate track
* Commonly paired as a joint concentration (Art/Film/Visual Studies, English, Anthropology, Classics, Romance Languages)

Architecture Studies Track

* A genuine distinct track within HAA, jointly administered with the Graduate School of Design (GSD)
* Own separate curriculum and requirements

## Human Biology, Behavior, and Evolution (HBBE, formerly HEB)

Basic Requirements: 13 courses (52 credits)

* LS 1a (or LPS A) and LS 1b
* 5 HEB courses (3 must cover Behavior, Evolution, and Anatomy/Physiology distribution)
* 4 additional courses in related fields (Integrative Biology, MCB, Neurobiology, Chemistry, Physical Sciences, Math, Statistics, or approved Psychology/History of Science courses)
* Sophomore tutorial (spring) + Junior research seminar
* No thesis required at basic level; honors based on concentration GPA

Human Evolutionary Biology / Mind, Brain, and Behavior Track: 15 courses (60 credits)

* LS 1b, Psychology 1 (or Gen Ed 1056: Human Nature), Gen Ed 1027, plus HEB courses
* 2 additional MBB courses + 3 additional related-field courses

## Human Developmental and Regenerative Biology (HDRB)

Concentration Requirements: single structural path

* Gateway: SCRB 10, then a molecular biology course (MCB 60, MCB 52, or SCRB 20)
* General chemistry: Physci 11 / Chem 10 / Chem 40 or equivalent
* At least 3 upper-level lecture/lab electives
* SCRB 91r (independent lab research) serves as the concentration tutorial, ordinarily junior year — mandatory for every concentrator, not just honors candidates
* Honors: requires SCRB 99 plus a submitted thesis

## Mathematics

Course Requirements: 12 letter-graded courses total

* 8 courses labeled Mathematics (Math Ma+Mb count as one combined credit) — at least 4 must be 100-level and satisfy breadth
* 4 courses in Mathematics or an approved related field (Applied Math, CS, Economics, Engineering Sciences, Physics, Applied Physics, Statistics)
* Breadth requirement: one course each in Analysis, Algebra, and Geometry/Topology
* Math Expository Requirement: a 5-page expository paper, due by end of third year
* One Freshman Seminar can substitute for one of the twelve courses, with DUS permission

Honors

* High or Highest Honors: requires a senior thesis
* Straight Honors: either a thesis, or four extra courses beyond the required twelve
* Department strongly urges everyone to write a thesis regardless

Joint Concentrations

* Honors-only by definition
* If Math is the allied field: 5 letter-graded Math courses, C- or higher, averaging B+, 3+ at the 100-level — no thesis, no expository paper required on the math side

## Molecular and Cellular Biology (MCB)

Concentration Requirements: single structural path (Basic/Honors)

* Foundational: LS 1a/1b
* Intermediate biology: MCB 60 + one of 63/64/65/68
* Upper-level biology: 2 courses, 1+ at the 100-level
* Chemistry: 1 general + 1 organic
* Math/computation: 1-2 half-courses (Math 19a+, or Stat 110/111, or CS50 combinations)
* Physics: mechanics + E&M, 1 half-course each
* Research: 1 semester minimum (LS 100r, MCB 91r, MCB 99, or approved summer research)
* Honors: requires an additional advanced course plus an additional organic chemistry course; thesis required specifically for highest honors eligibility

*Neuroscience is a separate "committee concentration" administratively housed within MCB, with its own distinct tracks (Cellular Neuroscience, and a broader Behavior/Cognition-adjacent track).*

## Philosophy

Basic Requirements: 11 courses (44 credits)

* One course each in Logic, Contemporary M&E, Ethics/Political/Aesthetics, History of Philosophy (C– or better)
* Two tutorials (PHIL 97 sophomore, PHIL 98 junior)
* 5 additional philosophy courses
* No thesis, no General Exam

Honors Eligibility (Thesis Track): 11 courses (44 credits)

* 3 distribution areas plus 2 history of philosophy courses
* 4 tutorial courses total (adds PHIL 99 senior tutorial)
* Thesis required — max 20,000 words, oral exam by two readers

Honors Eligibility (Non-Thesis Track): 12 courses (48 credits)

* Same distribution as Thesis Track, no thesis
* Only eligible for "Honors," not High/Highest Honors
* Requires 3.7+ honors GPA (weighted by year: 1.0/1.2/1.4/1.5 multiplier by class year)

Mind, Brain, and Behavior (MBB) Track: 15 courses (60 credits)

* 3 basic MBB courses (PSY 1, MCB 80, junior MBB seminar) + PHIL 156 + logic + 2 M&E courses + 2 ethics/history courses + 2 outside MBB electives
* Thesis required

Joint Concentrations: Philosophy as Primary: 8 Philosophy courses (36 credits)

* One course in each of the 4 areas + 4 additional philosophy courses
* Thesis required, relating to both fields, oral exam by two readers (one per department)

Joint Concentrations: Another Field as Primary: 6 Philosophy courses (24 credits)

* One course in 3 of the 4 areas + 3 additional philosophy courses
* Thesis required, directed in the primary field, one reader from Philosophy

## Physics

Basic Track

* Core requirements kept low to encourage interdisciplinary study

Honors Track: 13-15 courses

* Intro sequence + 2 math courses beyond multivariable/linear algebra + advanced laboratory class
* No thesis, tutorial, or general exam requirement (most students do Honors, since it's relatively unencumbered compared to other departments' honors programs)

Biophysics Track

* Specialized; honors-only unless second in a joint concentration

Applied Physics Track

* Specialized; honors-only unless second in a joint concentration

Chemistry and Physics (sibling concentration, same department)

* Separate concentration blending both fields; honors-only unless second in a joint

*Astrophysics is a fully separate concentration (not a Physics track), requiring Astronomy 16/17, Physics 15a-c, and a math sequence choice among 21a/b, 22a/b, or 25a/b.*

## Psychology

General Track (~70% of concentrators)

* Greatest flexibility; good preparation for business, law, medicine, or academia
* All students start with PSY 1 (or a Foundational Course if AP/IB-qualified) and PSY 1900 (statistics, by end of sophomore year)

Life Sciences: Cognitive Neuroscience & Evolutionary Psychology (CNEP) Track (~25% of concentrators)

* Combines Psychology with Life Sciences coursework

Mind/Brain/Behavior: Cognitive Science (MBB) Track (smallest group)

* Draws electives from Linguistics, Philosophy, HEB, History and Science, Neuroscience, and CS
* Requires a senior honors thesis — switching away from a thesis later requires switching tracks

General notes

* Joint concentrations are not permitted in Psychology
* Non-thesis track: minimum 10 courses count only toward Psychology; thesis track: minimum 12

## Sociology

Concentration Requirements: 12 courses, single structural path

* Introductory course (Sociol 1000)
* Sophomore Tutorial in Social Theory (Sociol 97)
* Models of Social Science Research (Sociol 1128, spring only)
* Quantitative Methods (Sociol 1156, or Stat 100/102/104/139 by petition)
* Junior Tutorial (Sociol 98) — independent research project, required of all concentrators
* Senior thesis always optional — required only for Honors (Sociol 99A/B)
* Honors GPA: 60% concentration GPA (best 8 of 12 letter grades) + thesis grades; High Honors requires A– GPA, Highest Honors requires A– GPA plus a Summa-range thesis grade

## Statistics

All tracks share a common core (multivariable calc + linear algebra to at least Math 19a/b, most commonly Math 21a/21b), then branch into one of:

* General Track — core statistical principles and methods
* Data Science Track
* Quantitative Finance Track (some sources list this as "Machine Learning Track" — terminology may vary by catalog year)
* Bioinformatics and Computational Biology (BCB) Track

All four tracks lead to the same degree (transcript does not distinguish). Stat 110, 111, and 139 must be letter-graded. Thesis required for honors eligibility and for joint concentrations; roughly half of graduating concentrators write one.

## Studies of Women, Gender, and Sexuality (WGS)

Concentration Requirements: single structural path (Regular / Thesis Track / Joint)

* Foundational courses: WGS 1200 (history approach) and/or WGS 1210 (theory approach)
* WOMGEN 97 ("Dreams of a Common Language") tutorial, usually by end of sophomore year
* 2 upper-level WGS seminars (1400+) or an Engaged Scholarship/Civic Engagement course
* 7 additional WGS-credit courses
* Thesis optional — juniors apply to a Thesis Track for honors or a joint concentration
* About half of concentrators pursue a joint concentration, drawing from an unusually wide range of allied fields
